What is the next best action for this account,
and can we safely automate it?

A small system that scales outbound prospecting without growing the SDR team at the same pace. Rules decide what must be exact, AI reads what people write, and every step is audited.

event→state→rules→ AI→decision→action→audit
Assumptions

What this demo assumes

LanguageThe case was written in English, so the system and this page are in English. Prospects reply in Spanish or Portuguese.
Data30 real companies from Brazil, Mexico and Colombia. Every person, email and tax ID is fictitious. CRM flags are simulated.
IntegrationsCRM, outreach and enrichment are mocks: they behave like real APIs, failures included, but nothing is ever sent.
Company existenceNo database knows every company in real time. A firm founded six months ago, with five partners and five employees, may already need spend control. Tax IDs are checked for format and check digit; existence would be confirmed by the tax authority or a data provider in production.
Company dataCountries, size, industry and legal type come from public registries through enrichment: CNPJ registry (CNAE, legal nature) in Brazil, SAT and DENUE in Mexico, RUES in Colombia.
CRMThere is no real CRM here. A JSON file plays Clara's CRM: step 1 loads it into SQLite (customers, AEs, opt-outs, recent outreach). The CRM owns each account_id; the system creates its own record the first time an event brings an id it does not know.
TimeSystems normally store time in UTC. For this demo, and to make it easier to follow, timestamps are shown in São Paulo time (UTC-3, no daylight saving). Every event carries its time zone, so comparisons stay correct with events from Mexico or Colombia.
ICPInferred from Clara's public positioning: companies with a legal entity in Brazil, Mexico or Colombia, from startups to enterprise. It lives in a config file that Growth can change.
Step 1

Event: something happens

Clara's CRM, outreach tool or calendar notifies the system through a webhook. Each event carries an event_id (to catch duplicates) and an occurred_at timestamp (to catch events that arrive out of order).

Step 2

State: what we know about each account

Every event updates the account in SQLite. Ownership flags (customer, assigned AE, opt-out) only come from the CRM, never from a webhook.

Step 3

Rules: deterministic, no AI by design

Anything with legal or business risk is decided by rules, in this order. The first match wins. The counter shows how many decisions each rule has taken since step 1 (every test you run adds to it).

    Step 4

    AI: reading what the prospect wrote

    Only replies reach the model, and only after the rules. Unsubscribe requests and prompt injection attempts are caught before. The output must pass four checks: schema, a quote that really appears in the reply, consistency and a confidence threshold. If any check fails, a person reviews it.
